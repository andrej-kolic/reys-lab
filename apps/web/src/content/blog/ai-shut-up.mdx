I have a friend, Richard. He's a great guy, interesting and funny, but he has one major
flaw: Richard is unable to summarize.

When he walks through the door of my apartment, I know I'll have to hear about everything
that happened to him that day, and since I last saw him, in full detail. With the names of
people and places, as if I'm supposed to know them just because he does. All in
chronological order, no exceptions! 45 minutes later, when the tank is empty, I can say
"hi" and we can have a conversation.

AI agents remind me of Richard a lot.

They make me say things like:
- why the fuck i have to read whole fucking novel only to figure out what the fuck is this about?
- shut up! explain this very fucking simply because it is very fucking simple for fucks sake!!!
- what is this? 1980s? and we don't have internet and shared fucking resources?

Fortunately, thanks to my friend, I have 10 years of experience handling a seemingly
unstoppable avalanche of word diarrhea, so I decided to do something about it.

## The problem
The first step was to document the specific behaviors that annoy me, so I made a list of
the things the agent does wrong:

- Writes too much and hides the answer in it
- Writes sentences that are hard to follow
- Uses words the user doesn't know, or keeps changing them
- Leaves its own work to the user
- Keeps breaking the same rules after being told to always follow them
- Asks without saying what it wants or why
- Explains in the abstract instead of showing
- Doesn't treat a question as a question
- Writes lists whose items don't say what they are
- Points to things the user can't see or identify
- Picks words that mislead
- Moves on without saying what it already did

## The goal
The second step was to figure out how to tame the agent into behaving the way I'd like.
Ideally, its communication style would:

- be clear and concise, measured against a set budget
- be structured: open with the answer or the core takeaway, use lists, and end with a
  clear conclusion or a direct question

In other words, it would optimize for the priciest resource of them all: my time and
attention.

## The implementation
The third step was the technical implementation. After some research, I decided to write
rules and skills that take over from them for a specific job. The communication rules are
two: `conversation-style` for structure, and `concision` for length and wording. A skill
like `x-write-docs` takes over from the `documentation` rule when the agent writes a
README. I use [rulesync](https://github.com/dyoshikawa/rulesync) to make them work in any
environment (I use Claude Code and Cursor), plus my own utility scripts and versioning.
You can check out my [Playbook](https://github.com/andrej-kolic/playbook) project (it has
grown since), but here is a basic example:

```md title=".rulesync/rules/concision.md" wrap
<!-- playbook:concision v11 (2026-10-23) — list items open with their point -->

# Concision

The measure of success is the reader's time: the least time spent parsing what you wrote and checking that it is true.

## Budgets

| Artifact         | Budget                                   |
|------------------|------------------------------------------|
| Chat reply       | 10 lines, until the reader asks for more |
| PR or issue body | 6 lines                                  |
| Review comment   | 3 lines                                  |
| Commit body      | 3 lines, or none                         |
```

```md title=".rulesync/rules/conversation-style.md" wrap
<!-- playbook:conversation-style v9 (2026-10-23) — item leads state their point -->

## Before responding

A question gets an answer. Only an explicit instruction — an imperative, a "yes", a direct request to act — gets a file edit, a commit, or any other change.

1. **Open with the answer or the core takeaway**; `concision` (The first line is contractual) holds the test it must pass.
```

```md title=".rulesync/skills/x-write-docs/SKILL.md" wrap
---
name: x-write-docs
description: Write or substantially update a README, tutorial, or how-to guide — pick the right structure per artifact type and follow README/tutorial writing conventions.
targets: ["claudecode", "cursor"]
---

<!-- playbook:x-write-docs v1 (2026-09-09) -->

Procedure for drafting or substantially rewriting documentation. Defer to the target project's own `documentation` rule (or an equivalent written convention) for which Diátaxis mode applies and for README-length/plain-language constraints — this skill is the checklist for actually writing the artifact once the mode is chosen, not a second copy of that rule.
```

## Next steps
One thing I noticed is that agents are pretty relaxed about deciding when to apply rules
and when to ignore (or deprioritize) them. So in longer conversations they slowly lean
back into their usual style, and I have to keep reminding them to "respect my authoritah".
I tried several things, such as writing "always respect the rules of communication" in
`AGENTS.md`. But that is a rule itself, and it gets suppressed to the depths of the
digital subconscious in no time. Claude Code's output style feature didn't improve it
either, and neither did its built-in memory.

So I'm going to try something different: a custom hook that adds a reminder of the rules
to every message I send, to refresh the agent's awareness. It will take some
experimenting, logging and debugging, and if it succeeds, a dedicated blog post.
